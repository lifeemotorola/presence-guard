KEYBLOOM — STORY CREDITS AND SOURCES

Story Typing contains 80 distinct complete historical stories, each at least
700 words. These are classic works, not stories newly written by KeyBloom or
Emmanuel Suah. They include folk tales, city life, nature and science fiction.
Some contain dated perspectives, hardship or violence; choose Another story if
one is not for you. These texts are public domain in the United States. Copyright
rules may differ elsewhere.

To make them usable with KeyBloom's US keyboard, curly quotation marks, dashes,
accents and other typography have been converted to printable characters;
line breaks and extra whitespace are flattened; illustration markers, footnote
markers and source notes are omitted. The story narratives are not shortened.
Word counts are based on whitespace-separated words after these adjustments.
The source copies below include the full original books and editorial notes.
Links are for reference; the app and its stories work offline.

Bryant's source notes describe The Gingerbread Man as a traditional telling,
with an ending suggested by Carolyn Bailey's For the Children's Hour; the little
Jackal stories as adapted from Old Deccan Days; Little Jack Rollaround as freely
adapted from Theodor Storm; The Blackberry-Bush as from Celia Thaxter's Stories
and Poems for Children; The Ten Fairies as adapted from H. A. Guerber; and The
Nightingale as adapted from Hans Christian Andersen. Bryant describes The Little
Fir Tree as her own story inspired by her childhood memory of Andersen's tale,
not as an adaptation of its text.

In the source edition's notes, Prince Hyacinth is attributed to Madame Leprince
de Beaumont; East of the Sun and West of the Moon, Why the Sea Is Salt, and
The Princess on the Glass Hill to Asbjornsen and Moe; Cinderella, Puss in Boots,
and Toads and Diamonds to Charles Perrault; Rumpelstiltzkin to Grimm; Felicia
to Madame d'Aulnoy; and The Black Bull of Norroway to Chambers' Popular
Traditions of Scotland. The Water-Lily. The Gold-Spinners is one continuous
story under that combined heading in the cited edition.
